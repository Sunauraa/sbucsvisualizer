All bounds are worst case. A recursive call's space is its stack, since each waiting frame holds its own variables.

You can rewrite any recursion as a loop plus a stack. The machine runs it that way.

Sometimes the recursive call is the last thing a function does. That's tail recursion, as in gcd. It doesn't need the stack, so a plain loop does the job in O(1) space.

Recursion wins on clarity for branching problems like Hanoi, trees and permutations. Iteration wins on space, and it won't hit StackOverflowError on deep inputs.

The three things every recursive method needs

1 · A base case

At least one input it answers without recursing. Skip it and you get infinite recursion. In Java, that means StackOverflowError.

2 · Progress toward it

Every recursive call needs a strictly smaller input: n−1, n/2, one character shorter. Otherwise you never reach the base case.

3 · Correct combination

Trust that the recursive call already works (the "recursive leap of faith"). Then combine its answer correctly. Induction proves that trust is safe.
