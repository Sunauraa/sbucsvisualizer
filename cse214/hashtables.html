Study notes

A hash table is a coat check: the hash turns your key into a ticket number, so finding it is Θ(1). The catch is two coats getting the same number, and every design here is a way to handle that.

Probes grow slowly with α, so the table can stay fuller.

Probes blow up as α → 1, so linear probing rehashes at α ≈ 0.5–0.7.

Quadratic probing comes close to this ideal; keep α < ½ so a free slot is guaranteed.

Things that bite people

composite m
clusters keys; use a prime, or a power of two with a well-mixed hash
deleting by blanking
breaks probe chains; you must use tombstones
tombstone build-up
searches slow down even though n is small; rehash to clear them
bad hash
e.g. summing characters: "cat" and "act" collide; multiply by 31 instead
mutable keys
change a key after inserting it and you can't find it again, because its hash moved
rehash cost
Θ(n + m) time and Θ(n + m) space, since every index depends on m

Try it: add random keys, delete a few, then search for a missing key and watch it walk through the tombstones.
